This SoK is based exclusively on previously published literature. No new experiments or data collection were conducted. All datasets originate from the surveyed works, and 
Responsibility for collection, anonymization, and ethical approval remains with the original authors. 
Accordingly, this survey introduces no additional ethical risks beyond those inherent in the referenced publications, except for the broader dual-use risks of dark jargon identification.

Methods for identifying coded and context-dependent language are inherently dual-use and transferable beyond criminal contexts. 
They could also target dissidents, journalists, activists, or marginalized communities in authoritarian settings, where similar linguistic strategies are used for protection. 
In such cases, these techniques may enable surveillance, censorship, or repression.% rather than harm prevention.

Future work should therefore acknowledge dual-use risks, clearly delimit intended applications, and incorporate safeguards such as human-in-the-loop analysis, conservative deployment assumptions, and human-rights assessments.
